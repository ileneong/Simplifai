## Top 3 this week

### 1. If you use Claude Pro, you just got a smarter assistant for the same price

- **What changed:** Anthropic launched Claude Opus 5 on July 24, 2026. It is now the strongest model available on Claude Pro and the new default on Claude Max, at no extra charge. Improvements are concentrated in complex reasoning, handling very long documents, and multi-step tasks that require thinking through several things at once. The context window is one million tokens, roughly 750,000 words, meaning you can feed it a full year of client records or a lengthy proposal in a single session.
- **Source + date:** Anthropic, anthropic.com/news, July 24, 2026.
- **So what for the owner:** If you already pay for Claude Pro ($20/month), your AI got meaningfully better this week without any action on your part. The payoff shows up most on your hardest tasks: a complex proposal, a long document you need summarised and acted on, a business problem with many moving parts. Try the model selector in Claude.ai and pick Opus 5 for your next tricky piece of work.
- **Honest caveat:** Opus 5 is not available on the free Claude plan. Free users get Sonnet 5, which handles everyday tasks well. Opus 5 earns its upgrade on complexity rather than speed, so if most of your Claude use is short prompts, the difference may feel modest.
- **Content angle:** Carousel, System teaching, hook: "If you use Claude Pro, you just got a smarter assistant for the same price."

### 2. You can now ask Google Docs to draw the diagram for you

- **What changed:** Gemini in Google Docs gained the ability to generate and edit images, diagrams, and infographics using plain text prompts, directly inside your document. Gemini reads the context of what you have already written, generates a relevant visual, and lets you update multiple visuals at once with a single instruction. Rollout began July 28, 2026.
- **Source + date:** Google, Google Workspace Updates blog (workspaceupdates.googleblog.com), July 28, 2026.
- **So what for the owner:** Service menus, program guides, client proposals, and intake forms all benefit from a clear visual or diagram. Previously that meant switching to a design tool, finding a template, or paying someone. Now you describe what you need in plain English and it appears alongside your text.
- **Honest caveat:** This requires a paid Google Workspace plan that includes Gemini (Business Standard or above, currently from $14 USD per user per month) or a Google AI Pro subscription. It is not on the free Google account or basic Workspace plans. If you already pay for Google Workspace, check your Gemini settings before assuming you have access.
- **Content angle:** Carousel, System teaching, hook: "You can now ask Google Docs to draw the diagram. Here is what that looks like in practice."

### 3. Ask your booking calendar a question, in plain English

- **What changed:** Calendly updated its AI assistant, Callie, on July 30, 2026. In beta, Callie lets you query your Calendly data with natural language: which clients rescheduled this month, what your busiest booking days have been, which appointment types are running longest. You type a question and Callie finds the answer without manual report-pulling.
- **Source + date:** Calendly, Calendly Help Centre (calendly.com/help), July 30, 2026.
- **So what for the owner:** For any appointment-based business (a clinic, a coach, a studio, a trades business, a consultant), your booking calendar holds patterns that matter. Spotting them usually means exporting to a spreadsheet and digging through rows. Callie does that lookup in seconds and returns a plain-language answer.
- **Honest caveat:** Callie is in beta and currently available to select users only. Plan requirements have not been confirmed publicly. It works only with data inside Calendly and cannot pull from other tools. Availability will expand over the coming months.
- **Content angle:** Single post or short Reel, System teaching, hook: "Ask your booking calendar who rescheduled this month. No spreadsheet needed."
